Brain games, Greek & Roman

Short challenges for memory, attention, speed and spatial thinking, set among mosaics, coins, aqueducts and oracles. Each game watches how you are doing: it gets harder after a success and eases off after a miss. A session takes one to three minutes.

What these games can and can’t do

They are fun challenges for memory, attention, speed and spatial thinking, and you will see your scores climb as you practice. That is real progress, but mostly at the game itself: getting better at Coin Match makes you better at Coin Match. We don’t claim that any game here raises intelligence, protects against memory loss, or carries over to school, work or sport. In 2016 a large brain-training company agreed to pay $2 million to settle U.S. Federal Trade Commission charges that its ads made claims like those (FTC press release).

For the full story of how practice changes performance, read about Ayumu the chimpanzee on the Numbers in Order page.
